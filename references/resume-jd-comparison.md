# Resume/JD Comparison Context

Use this reference before selecting, auditing, or tailoring a resume for a job. It provides two optional screening audits. Treat it as a working strategy, not as a verified claim about any named company's internal hiring system.

Use the matching standard in `resume-job-matching-and-tailoring.md` before either screening-path audit. For roles requiring ownership, the audits must not upgrade methodological adjacency into domain-lever ownership. For internships and entry-level roles, evaluate evidence appropriate to their stated requirements.

## Classify the Likely First Screen

Choose one of three working classifications:

1. `AI-first / frontier`: the company is likely to use AI-assisted review or highly automated ranking.
2. `Traditional ATS-heavy`: the company is likely to depend heavily on conventional parsing and keyword/rule matching.
3. `Unknown / mixed`: evidence is insufficient or the process likely combines both.

Do not state that a specific employer definitely uses a particular system without evidence. For `Unknown / mixed`, run both paths.

## Path A — AI-First / Frontier Review

Review the resume together with the full JD from the perspective of a skeptical first-pass hiring reviewer.

1. Extract the JD's title, level, core responsibilities, required capabilities, domain, business problem, and stated outcomes.
2. Identify likely red flags, including unclear level fit, unexplained scope, weak evidence, unsupported keyword claims, chronology ambiguity, generic impact language, or a large gap between the JD and the resume's foregrounded story.
3. For every material concern, identify what evidence would resolve it.
4. Search the candidate's approved resume and deep-evidence sources for truthful support before concluding that the evidence is missing.
5. Add or foreground concise clues that make the relevant evidence machine-readable and easy to infer: scope, ownership, metric, decision, stakeholder, system, domain, or outcome.
6. Re-run the audit and distinguish resolved concerns from genuine remaining gaps.

The goal is not to write for a machine at the expense of people. The resume should survive an automated first pass and remain concise, credible, and readable for the human reviewer who follows.

## Path B — Traditional ATS-Heavy Review

Optimize for reliable parsing and responsibility-level language alignment.

1. Derive keywords and phrases primarily from the JD's responsibilities and qualifications, not only from its technology stack.
2. Map each important phrase to evidence in `Professional Experience`. Keywords that appear only in `Skills` should not be treated as sufficient proof of experience.
3. Where truthful, align action verbs, responsibility phrasing, domain nouns, and outcome language with the JD. Similar sentence openings may improve literal matching, but do not copy a responsibility unless the candidate actually performed it.
4. Preserve natural, specific accomplishment bullets. Integrate terms into evidence-bearing statements instead of keyword stuffing.
5. Check that the most important matched concepts occur in high-signal sections and near the strongest relevant evidence.
6. Use a simple, parseable layout. Prefer common punctuation, spaces, periods, and line breaks. Avoid unusual separators, decorative glyphs, text boxes, complex multi-column reading order, or other constructs that may be parsed inconsistently.
7. Treat LaTeX as an implementation choice, not an automatic rejection. If used, verify the exported PDF's selectable text, reading order, section labels, dates, and bullet extraction.

## Comparison Output

For each job, produce or record:

- likely screening path and confidence;
- top JD signals;
- strongest resume evidence;
- material red flags or gaps;
- responsibility-level keyword matches and misses;
- formatting or parsing risks;
- whether an existing PDF is strong enough;
- exact truthful edits recommended or made;
- unresolved gaps that must not be fabricated.
- the role's single core lever, evidence classification, and the resulting match-tier ceiling.

Prefer an existing strong PDF when it passes the applicable audit. Otherwise use the closest editable base, create a new versioned artifact, export it to PDF, and verify parsing before submission.

## Evidence and Safety Guardrails

- Never fabricate experience, ownership, metrics, tools, credentials, or outcomes to match a JD.
- A model's red flag is a hypothesis to investigate, not proof that the resume is defective.
- A literal keyword match is useful only when supported by the candidate's actual work.
- Company-category assumptions are routing heuristics and should be revised when the application flow provides better evidence.
