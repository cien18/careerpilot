# Resume / Job Matching & Tailoring

Read this reference before sourcing, ranking, recommending, selecting a resume for, or tailoring to a role for the candidate. Use the detailed framework primarily as internal reasoning; keep ordinary user-facing results concise.

## Core-Lever Test — For Roles Requiring Ownership

Use this test when the job expects the candidate to own or materially drive a business, product, or operating outcome. For internships and entry-level roles that do not require prior ownership, assess the stated skills, coursework, projects, internships, and learning potential instead. Do not impose a senior-level ownership requirement on a junior role.

Before ranking a role highly, identify the **single business or product lever** the hiring manager is hiring this person to own. Require direct evidence that the candidate previously owned or materially drove the same lever or an operationally equivalent one.

Methodological adjacency alone—similar modeling, experimentation, ROI, causal-inference, or analytics techniques—is not sufficient for a top-tier match when the role expects deep domain-specific ownership.

Apply the gate in this order:

1. State the lever as an operating outcome, not a method or broad function. Examples include creator activation and publishing, infrastructure-capacity allocation, model release reliability, developer trust and feedback resolution, marketing attribution and acquisition efficiency, marketplace pricing, lifecycle retention, or AI-product evaluation.
2. Identify what the hire must repeatedly decide, change, or improve through that lever, plus the durable outputs expected: roadmap decisions, experiments, launch gates, models, operating metrics, investment allocation, workflows, or cross-functional mechanisms.
3. Find evidence that the candidate owned or materially drove the same lever. An operational equivalent must share the underlying decision owner, operating loop, constraints, and business outcome—not merely the analytical technique.
4. Classify the evidence:
   - `Direct`: same lever and materially similar operating context.
   - `Operationally equivalent`: different surface domain but substantially the same decision system, ownership loop, and outcome.
   - `Methodologically adjacent`: similar methods without equivalent domain ownership.
   - `No evidence`: no defensible match in the approved sources.
5. Cap the provisional rating: only `Direct` or well-supported `Operationally equivalent` evidence can earn a top-tier match. `Methodologically adjacent` roles may remain plausible or exploratory, but must not be presented as top-tier based only on current documents.
6. If the role otherwise looks worthwhile but the approved evidence does not establish the core lever, do not assume the candidate lacks the experience. Keep the opportunity alive, state the one or two material ownership gaps concisely, and ask whether she has done the specific work. Search deeper approved sources first when practical so the user is not asked about facts already recorded.
7. If the candidate confirms the missing experience, locate or request enough factual detail to substantiate it, update the evidence mapping, reassess the match, and tailor the resume to surface that experience. A one-page resume is editable evidence, not a fixed boundary.

Do not rename a method into a business lever to manufacture equivalence. Do not infer ownership from keyword overlap. If the JD spans several responsibilities, still choose the one lever most likely to determine hiring success and treat the others as secondary signals.

## Matching Logic

### Decode hiring logic before keywords

Start from the team's real decision system and operating model. Determine what the hire must own and which outputs the team needs: metrics, experiments, models, evaluation systems, launch criteria, prioritization systems, operating dashboards, roadmap decisions, or cross-functional mechanisms.

### Reconstruct from the full project inventory

Current resume bullets are compressed evidence, not the full experience record. Absence from a resume is not proof that the candidate lacks an experience. Use the richest approved sources: interview prep, implementation plans, project notes, recent resumes, self-reviews, capability maps, project handoffs, and startup materials. Reconstruct the strongest coherent, truthful project story. When a material gap remains unresolved, ask the candidate a narrow, role-specific question instead of silently rejecting the role.

### Compare with the actual candidate pool

Ask both whether the candidate can do the job and whether she has a structural advantage relative to likely strong applicants. Prefer roles where the candidate has a documented advantage over likely strong applicants. Do not let a rare cross-domain profile override failure of the Core-Lever Test.

### Match the senior-level action chain

When supported, map evidence across: problem framing and decision definition; success metrics; logging, telemetry, or data model; data and method validation; analysis, experiment, model, or evaluation; cross-functional execution; product, roadmap, launch, or investment decision; and productization into a durable operating system, monitor, framework, dashboard, or workflow.

### Tailor the evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest current resume as the structural base. Tailoring changes which projects lead, how ownership is framed, and which evidence is emphasized—not merely keywords. Preserve strong structure, formatting, paper links, and current bullets unless materially richer evidence exists.

### Represent the largest defensible scope

When the candidate materially drove product direction, roadmap, operating model, experiment, evaluation, alignment, implementation, or launch, represent that senior-IC scope even when PM, Engineering, or other partners contributed. Use ownership language supported by the record; do not claim sole ownership when the evidence shows shared work.

## User-Facing Output

Do the full hiring-manager and Core-Lever analysis internally. Do not routinely print every intermediate field or a long checklist. For ordinary sourcing and matching, output only what helps the user decide:

- If current evidence already supports a strong match, recommend the role for application and briefly identify the decisive fit. Do not narrate the whole framework.
- If the role is promising but a material lever-ownership question remains, preserve the opportunity, name the gap concisely, and ask whether the candidate has done that specific work.
- If a confirmed hard gap makes the role materially uncompetitive, explain the decisive reason briefly rather than listing every mismatch.
- Provide a deeper hiring-manager analysis only when the user asks for it or when the decision is genuinely ambiguous and the added detail changes what to do.

Do not confuse a top-tier label with permission to apply. Strongly matched roles should move forward under the user's application-authorization rules. Plausible roles with manageable gaps may also be worth applying to after the best truthful resume positioning; the test is for honest prioritization, not needless opportunity elimination.

## Resume-Tailoring Standard

- Preserve formatting, paper links, and core identity unless explicitly asked otherwise.
- Use the newest, strongest supported fact pattern.
- Lead with evidence of the role's core lever, then supporting methods and transferable skills.
- Elevate product ownership, system design, decision influence, cross-functional execution, and operating impact.
- Avoid shrinking broad product or program systems into isolated dashboards, pipelines, or analyses.
- Keep claims ambitious but grounded in the complete approved project record.
